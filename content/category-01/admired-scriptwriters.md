### What stands out about them

 First, Emma Thompson: the screenwriter of one of my favorite films of all time, *Sense and Sensibility*. Emma had the incredible challenge of adapting Jane Austen's novel to a film and did it with more grace and genius than I ever could. She went above and beyond to influence decisions made for costumes, location, and even the director they selected to lead the project. Her empathy helps her strike the perfect balance between satire and comedy.

Sara Davidson isn't a very well-known writer, but the episodes she wrote for Dr. Quinn, Medicine Woman fill many of my childhood memories. As I've grown, I've appreciated her work more and more. Dr. Quinn did what many shows wish to do. It discussed societal issues with the perfect balance of lovable characters and realistic life struggles. To this day, I haven't found a show that hits the nail on the head with every single episode in the way this show did. It will forever offer a blueprint for my own scriptwriting. 

Jon Erwin is a new favorite for me. I have seen many of his films, such as *Woodlawn* and *I Can Only Imagine*, but it was his most recent project that made me recognize his true talent. *How of David* is a massive success on Prime Video, and Jon Erwin's writing plays a huge role in that. I am always impressed by writers who do more than just write. Jon Erwin is also a producer and co-founder of his production company, Wonder Project
 
>"Just write. It doesn't matter what you write. Just sit at that desk and write." - Emma Thompson
